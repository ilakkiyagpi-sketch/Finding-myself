# Habits, Routines & Time

## Core idea for the app
Mothers' days are unpredictable, so rigid routines fail and cause guilt.
Design **identity-based, tiny, anchored, flexible** habits with a
"minimum version" for bad days, and look at time **honestly** (actual vs
imagined), across the whole week rather than one day.

## Source books

| Book | Author (year) | Key idea to use |
|---|---|---|
| *Atomic Habits* | James Clear (2018) | **Identity-based habits**: every action is a vote for the person you want to be. Four laws: make it obvious, attractive, easy, satisfying. Habit stacking. "Never miss twice." |
| *Tiny Habits* | BJ Fogg (2019) | Behaviour = Motivation × Ability × Prompt. Start tiny, anchor to an existing routine ("After I … I will …"), celebrate immediately to wire the habit. |
| *The Power of Habit* | Charles Duhigg (2012) | Habit loop: cue → routine → reward; change the routine, keep cue and reward. Keystone habits ripple into other areas. |
| *Better Than Before* / *The Four Tendencies* | Gretchen Rubin (2015 / 2017) | People respond to expectations differently: **Upholder, Questioner, Obliger, Rebel**. Obligers (common among mothers) need outer accountability, even for self-care. |
| *168 Hours* | Laura Vanderkam (2010) | Think in weeks (168 h), not days; track time to find hidden hours. |
| *I Know How She Does It* | Laura Vanderkam (2015) | Time diaries of working mothers show more personal time than they feel they have; life is a mosaic of tiles, not a balance scale. |
| *Four Thousand Weeks* | Oliver Burkeman (2021) | Finite time: you'll never get everything done; choose consciously what to neglect; embrace limits. |
| *Essentialism* | Greg McKeown (2014) | Less but better; a clear "no" to non-essentials protects the vital few. |
| *Deep Work* | Cal Newport (2016) | Protected, focused blocks produce meaningful work; relevant to protecting creative "unicorn space". |
| *Sacred Rest* | Saundra Dalton-Smith (2017) | Seven types of rest: physical, mental, emotional, social, sensory, creative, spiritual. |

## Routine Analyser (what to measure)
- **Time diary** (quick-log or calendar import): category, role, energy +/-, "for me / for others".
- **Energy curve:** when is she most alert? (chronotype)
- **Load ratio:** % of waking time serving others vs. self vs. shared.
- **Values alignment:** link to `values-strengths-purpose`.
- **Rest deficit:** which of the 7 rest types is missing.
- **Tendency:** Rubin quiz → shapes reminders/accountability style.

## Routine Setter (how to design)
1. Choose identity statement: "I am a woman who reads / moves / creates."
2. Pick a **tiny** behaviour (≤2 min version) + an **anchor** from her existing day (after school drop, during baby nap, after evening tea).
3. Define **three levels**: *Minimum* (bad day), *Normal*, *Stretch*.
4. **Season mode:** newborn / toddler / school-age / exam season / festival / sick-week — routines scale down automatically.
5. **Never miss twice** messaging instead of streak-shame.
6. Weekly view (168 h), with at least one protected self block.
7. Celebrate small wins (Fogg) with micro-animation/affirmation.

## Anti-patterns to avoid in the app
- Guilt-inducing streak counters that reset to zero.
- 5 a.m. "productivity" defaults.
- Comparing her to other users.
- Routines that ignore caregiving reality.
