# Matrescence & Motherhood Identity

## Core idea for the app
Becoming a mother is a **developmental life stage**, like adolescence — not a
problem to fix. Body, brain, relationships, career, values and self-image all
reorganise. Feeling "lost" is a normal sign of transition, not failure. The
app's job is to help her **integrate** the old self and the mother self, not
choose between them.

## Source books (summarised in our own words)

| Book | Author (year) | Key idea to use |
|---|---|---|
| *The Tender Gift: Breastfeeding* | Dana Raphael (1973) | Coined the word **matrescence**: the passage of becoming a mother, paralleling adolescence. |
| *Matrescence: On Pregnancy, Childbirth and Motherhood* | Lucy Jones (2023) | Matrescence is biological, psychological and social at once; the brain physically changes; society under-recognises the upheaval, which deepens isolation. |
| *Motherhood: Facing and Finding Yourself* | Lisa Marchiano (2021) | Jungian lens: motherhood is an initiation that strips the old ego and can make you *more* yourself if you meet the hard feelings (ambivalence, rage, grief) honestly. |
| *The Birth of a Mother* | Daniel Stern & Nadia Bruschweiler-Stern (1998) | A "motherhood mindset" forms: new preoccupations (keeping baby alive, being validated, re-shaping identity). Identity is re-organised, not erased. |
| *What No One Tells You* | Alexandra Sacks & Catherine Birndorf (2019) | Normalises ambivalence: the push–pull between wanting to be close to the baby and missing your old freedom is expected, not pathological. |
| *Mama Rising* | Amy Taylor-Kabbaz (2019) | Matrescence as a rite of passage that deserves ritual, support and self-recognition. |
| *Of Woman Born* | Adrienne Rich (1976) | Separates motherhood as *experience* from motherhood as *institution* (social expectations). Many "shoulds" are cultural, not personal. |
| *A Life's Work* | Rachel Cusk (2001) | Honest memoir of how motherhood disrupts the writer-self; validates the loss of the former self. |
| *Fair Play* | Eve Rodsky (2019) | Invisible domestic labour as a card system: each task has Conception, Planning, Execution. Owning whole cards reduces mental load. |
| *Find Your Unicorn Space* | Eve Rodsky (2021) | Everyone needs protected time for a self-expressive pursuit beyond work and caregiving — "unicorn space" — shared with others and goal-oriented. |
| *The Fourth Trimester* | Kimberly Ann Johnson (2017) | Postpartum recovery is long; body-based care and support are foundations for re-finding self. |
| *The Postnatal Depletion Cure* | Oscar Serrallach (2018) | Long-term physical/nutritional depletion after birth affects mood, energy and identity; rest and replenishment matter. |
| *Good Inside* | Becky Kennedy (2022) | "Two things are true" thinking and repair — useful for mothers' self-talk: I can be a good mother *and* struggle. |
| *Untamed* | Glennon Doyle (2020) | Women are conditioned to abandon their inner knowing to meet expectations; reclaiming self = listening to that inner voice again. |
| Research: Aurélie Athan (Columbia) | — | Academic revival of matrescence; frames it as potential for growth, not just risk. |

## Recurring themes → app design
1. **Ambivalence is normal** → AI never shames mixed feelings; validate "both/and".
2. **Grief for the former self** → Rewind module lets her honour who she was.
3. **Invisible labour** → Roles module maps conception/planning/execution load.
4. **Unicorn space** → every weekly plan protects at least one small block for *her*.
5. **Body first** → check-ins include sleep, recovery, nourishment, not just mood.
6. **Institution vs experience** → reflection prompts that separate "what I'm told a mother should be" from "what kind of mother and person I choose to be".
7. **Rite of passage** → milestones and gentle rituals ("letter to my pre-mother self").

## Reflection prompts the app can use
- What did a normal Saturday look like for you before children? What part of it do you miss most — the activity, or the feeling it gave you?
- Which parts of motherhood feel like *you*? Which feel like a costume?
- What "should" about being a mother did you inherit? Do you agree with it?
- Name one thing you used to do just for joy. What is a 15-minute version of it?
- What has motherhood *given* you (skills, strengths, values) that the old you didn't have?

## Tone rules
Warm, non-judgemental, never toxic-positive. Avoid "enjoy every moment".
Acknowledge cultural context (joint families, in-law expectations, working vs
stay-at-home pressures) without assuming. Screen for postpartum depression
signals — see `wellbeing-safety-guardrails`.
