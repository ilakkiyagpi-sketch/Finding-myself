# Self-Awareness & Self-Reflection

## Core idea for the app
Reflection only helps when it produces **insight + kindness**, not rumination.
Ask *what* questions more than *why* questions, write things down, and pair
honesty with self-compassion.

## Source books

| Book | Author (year) | Key idea to use |
|---|---|---|
| *Insight* | Tasha Eurich (2017) | Two kinds of self-awareness: **internal** (knowing your values, feelings, patterns) and **external** (how others see you). Most people overestimate theirs. Ask "**What** am I feeling / what matters?" not "**Why** am I like this?" — "why" invites rumination. |
| *Emotional Intelligence* | Daniel Goleman (1995) | Self-awareness is the base of EI: recognise emotions as they happen, then self-regulate, motivate, empathise, relate. |
| *Mindsight* | Daniel J. Siegel (2010) | Observing your own mind with openness and curiosity; "name it to tame it" — labelling emotions calms them. |
| *The Gifts of Imperfection* | Brené Brown (2010) | Wholehearted living: let go of who you think you're supposed to be; embrace who you are. Guideposts include self-compassion, rest, play, creativity. |
| *Daring Greatly* / *Atlas of the Heart* | Brené Brown (2012 / 2021) | Vulnerability as courage; shame thrives in secrecy. *Atlas* gives a vocabulary of ~87 emotions — useful for mood tagging. |
| *Self-Compassion* | Kristin Neff (2011) | Three components: **self-kindness**, **common humanity**, **mindfulness**. Self-compassion increases motivation more than self-criticism. |
| *Radical Acceptance* | Tara Brach (2003) | RAIN practice: **R**ecognise, **A**llow, **I**nvestigate with kindness, **N**urture. |
| *When Things Fall Apart* | Pema Chödrön (1997) | Staying with discomfort instead of fleeing it; groundlessness as a doorway. |
| *The Evolving Self* | Robert Kegan (1982) | Adults keep developing new "orders of mind" — from being defined by others' expectations (socialised mind) to authoring one's own values (self-authoring mind). Motherhood often triggers this shift. |
| *The Artist's Way* | Julia Cameron (1992) | **Morning pages** (3 pages of free writing) and the weekly **artist date** (solo time to refill creativity). |
| *Opening Up by Writing It Down* | James Pennebaker & Joshua Smyth (1990 / 2016) | **Expressive writing** research: 15–20 min writing about emotional experiences for a few days improves wellbeing; best when writing moves toward meaning/insight words over time. |
| *Why Has Nobody Told Me This Before?* | Julie Smith (2022) | Practical CBT/ACT-based tools for low mood, anxiety, self-criticism. |

## App mechanics derived
- **Daily Mirror check-in (≤60s):** mood with emotion granularity (Brown/Barrett), energy, one sentence "what mattered today".
- **Prompt engine rule:** prefer *what/how* prompts. If the user loops on *why*, the AI gently reframes: "What would help right now?"
- **RAIN flow** for heavy moments (4 guided screens).
- **Self-compassion break** (Neff): "This is hard. Others feel this too. May I be kind to myself."
- **Morning pages mode:** free-write, private, no AI analysis unless she asks.
- **External awareness:** optional "trusted mirror" — invite a friend/partner to answer 3 questions about her strengths (Eurich's 360 idea, made gentle).
- **Weekly synthesis:** AI summarises patterns across entries ("You felt most yourself on days you walked alone").

## Prompt bank
- What am I feeling right now — can I find a more precise word?
- When did I feel most like myself today? Least?
- What did I need today that I didn't get? What's one small way to get it tomorrow?
- If my best friend described this week to me, what would I tell her?
- What am I pretending not to know?
