# Narrative Identity & Life Story ("Rewind")

## Core idea for the app
We understand who we are through the **story** we tell about our life. When a
big change happens (marriage, motherhood, migration, loss), the story breaks.
Re-telling it — chapters, turning points, recurring themes — reconnects the
past self to the present and lets her **author** the next chapter.

## Source books

| Book | Author (year) | Key idea to use |
|---|---|---|
| *The Stories We Live By* | Dan P. McAdams (1993) | Identity = an internalised, evolving life story. **Life Story Interview**: life chapters, key scenes (high point, low point, turning point, earliest memory), challenges, personal ideology, future script. Themes of **agency** (power, achievement) and **communion** (love, connection). |
| *The Redemptive Self* | Dan P. McAdams (2006) | **Redemption sequences** (bad → good) are linked to wellbeing; **contamination sequences** (good → spoiled) to lower wellbeing. Helping people find meaning in hard scenes is protective. |
| *Life Is in the Transitions* | Bruce Feiler (2020) | "Lifequakes" — major disruptions; most people face several. Transitions have phases: the long goodbye, the messy middle, the new beginning. Shedding old habits/mindsets is part of it. |
| *Transitions* | William Bridges (1980) | Change is external; transition is internal. Three phases: **Ending** (letting go), **Neutral Zone** (confusing in-between), **New Beginning**. |
| *Working Identity* | Herminia Ibarra (2003) | You find a new self by **doing** small experiments, not only by introspecting. Test "possible selves" in action. |
| *Possible Selves* (paper) | Hazel Markus & Paula Nurius (1986) | We carry images of selves we hope to become and fear becoming; these guide motivation. |
| *Maps of Narrative Practice* | Michael White (2007) | Narrative therapy: separate the person from the problem ("externalising"); find "unique outcomes" that contradict the problem story; re-author. |
| *Man's Search for Meaning* | Viktor Frankl (1946) | Meaning can be found through work/deeds, love, and the attitude taken toward unavoidable suffering. |
| *The Power of Meaning* | Emily Esfahani Smith (2017) | Four pillars of meaning: **belonging, purpose, storytelling, transcendence**. |
| *Maybe You Should Talk to Someone* | Lori Gottlieb (2019) | We all edit our stories; therapy helps rewrite them; "the past is not fixed in its meaning". |
| *The Heroine's Journey* | Maureen Murdock (1990) | Feminine version of the hero's journey: separating from the feminine, chasing outer success, descent/disillusion, reclaiming and integrating the feminine and masculine. |
| *Women Who Run With the Wolves* | Clarissa Pinkola Estés (1992) | Myths and stories about reclaiming the instinctive, "wild" self that social roles suppress. |

## App mechanics derived — the Rewind module
1. **Life chapters:** she divides her life into 4–8 chapters and names each (e.g., "The college dreamer", "First job in Chennai", "Becoming Amma").
2. **Key scenes per chapter:** high point, low point, turning point, a moment she felt fully herself.
3. **Then-Me profile:** for each chapter, tag traits, passions, skills, friends, places, music, clothes, deeds she was proud of.
4. **Theme detection (AI):** agency vs communion balance per chapter; redemption vs contamination framing; recurring values.
5. **Bridges phase tracker:** "Where are you now — ending, neutral zone, or new beginning?"
6. **Externalising:** name the problem ("The Fog", "The Guilt Voice") so she relates *to* it rather than *as* it.
7. **Unique outcomes:** AI surfaces moments that contradict "I've lost myself" ("Last Tuesday you stayed up to sketch — that's the artist in you").
8. **Possible-self experiments (Ibarra):** turn a past passion into a tiny real-world test.

## Prompt bank
- If your life were a book, what would the chapter titles be?
- Describe a moment when you felt completely, unmistakably yourself. Where were you? What were you doing?
- What did 20-year-old you believe she would be doing now? What still feels true?
- What did you let go of to get here? Which of those do you want back, in some form?
- Tell a hard moment from your life — and then tell what it taught you or gave you.
